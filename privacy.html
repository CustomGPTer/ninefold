Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026. This policy covers the Ninefold app for Android (package uk.jj.ninefold) and this website. Ninefold is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Your games, statistics, achievements and settings are kept on your phone. They are never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • Ninefold goes online only when you tap Check for updates. A race with a friend talks only to their phone, over your wifi.
  • Photos of puzzles are read on your phone and are not kept or uploaded.
  • Copies leave the app only when you make them: a backup file or a shared puzzle code.

What stays on your phone

Ninefold keeps its files in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. They are left out of phone cloud backups.

  • Games in progress, including their moves, notes and colours.
  • Statistics and the history of finished games: when, which puzzle type and level, the time, mistakes, hints, score and race placing.
  • Daily challenge results, event progress and achievements.
  • Your settings, including your name for races with a friend.

When Ninefold uses the network

Ninefold holds Android's internet permission for two things only.

  • Check for updates. Only when you tap it, Ninefold asks GitHub, where it is published, for the latest version. If you choose to download it, the app file comes from GitHub. GitHub sees an ordinary web request, including your IP address, as any website would. Nothing about you or your games is sent. GitHub's own privacy statement covers what it keeps.
  • Racing a friend. When you host or join a race, the two phones connect to each other directly over your wifi network. They exchange your names, the chosen puzzle and progress during the race. Nothing goes to the internet or to the developer.

Opening More apps or the website opens your own browser.

Photos of puzzles

When you photograph a puzzle or pick a photo, Ninefold reads it on the phone with a text-recognition model from Google's ML Kit that is built into the app. The photo is not uploaded and not kept after reading. ML Kit's own usage reporting is switched off in Ninefold, so nothing is sent.

Taking a photo uses your phone's own camera app. Ninefold does not hold camera permission.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: the update check, and finding a friend's phone on your wifi.
  • Install apps: handing a downloaded update to Android's installer. You confirm every install.
  • Notifications: the daily reminder, only if you switch it on.
  • Run at start-up: setting the reminder again after your phone restarts.
  • Vibration: gentle feedback while you play.

Your choices

Because nothing is sent to the developer, there is nothing for the developer to show you or delete. You can delete everything by uninstalling Ninefold, or by clearing its storage in your phone's settings. Settings in the app also lets you reset statistics.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking. GitHub may keep server logs, as described in its own privacy statement.
